New Orleans: BLU reports versus bike-lane tickets

The map data was recovered successfully. The earlier report's missing-data conclusion is superseded. Inspecting the working browser map revealed a separate public BLU pin-data file, despite the map-page URL returning HTTP 404 to direct requests.

Main finding

Bienville Avenue, Banks Street, Allen Toussaint Boulevard, and North Galvez Street are the strongest recent enforcement-mismatch candidates. They have repeated vehicle-obstruction reports with little or no recorded enforcement under the specific bicycle-lane parking code.

Comparison period: January 1, 2025–July 31, 2026, the latest useful overlap with the previously extracted city tickets. BLU reports after July are excluded. Street totals below count only confident geographic matches; a distinct date means a calendar date in the map's date field, not a unique reporter.

Extraction and geographic matching

  1. Identified the data URLs in the resource list of the user's working BLU map frame. The all-cities source downloaded contains 139,900 records; the already-loaded browser map displayed an older total of 138,066. The analysis uses the downloaded snapshot, not the displayed cached total.
  2. Selected cityKey = new-orleans-la: 1,588 records, with source dates from January 31, 2020 to September 17, 2026 where present. Preserved the original features in blu_new_orleans.geojson.
  3. Of those records, 1,468 are vehicle categories: 956 private-owner, 327 company, 113 taxi/Uber/livery/Lyft, and 72 municipal/USPS. Excluded 56 construction and 64 other-condition records from the parking comparison. Vehicle categories are a proxy; individual photos have not been adjudicated for ticket eligibility.
  4. There are 14 records without coordinates, 41 without dates, and one outlying coordinate in California despite a New Orleans city label. Missing dates are excluded from period comparisons. Missing/outlying coordinates are excluded from spatial rankings. The city label is a metro label; it is not an official municipal-boundary test.
  5. Retrieved 28,526 city road-centerline features from the City of New Orleans road service, using its public geometry, street names, and address ranges.
  6. Matched each valid coordinate to its nearest road polyline in an approximate local meter projection centered on latitude 30°. A confident match requires distance ≤30 meters and the nearest different street to be at least 5 meters farther away. Intersection ties/near-ties are marked ambiguous and excluded from headline counts. This threshold describes match quality, not certainty about the original incident location or existence of a bike lane.
  7. Assigned a report to a hundred-block only when all positive address-range endpoints on that road feature fall within the same hundred-block. No invented address interpolation is used in the report tables.
  8. Compared normalized street names against the previously extracted 3,727 distinct tickets under code 154.923(17). The recent period contains 538 tickets. Original ticket-location strings remain available for audit. Other violation codes are not included.

Within the comparison period, BLU has 484 vehicle reports: 383 confident street matches, 78 ambiguous intersection matches, 18 farther than 30 meters from a mapped centerline, and 5 without coordinates. These classes reconcile to the total. All qualifying records, including excluded matches and their distances, are saved in blu_reports_matched.json.

Basic spelling/abbreviation normalization expands street suffixes, compass prefixes, and St./Saint. Additional matching normalizes punctuation and A P/AP Tureaud; Guillaume Place is combined with Joseph Guillaume Place. Raw names are retained. The leading recent zero-ticket candidates were also checked against raw ticket strings: no recent Bienville Street/Avenue, Allen Toussaint, or Lee variants were found. Historical name changes and other spelling errors can still affect secondary streets.

Sensitivity checks

Tightening the maximum road distance from 30 meters to 15 meters, while retaining the 5-meter alternative-street margin, gives:

| Street | Reports at 30 m | Reports at 15 m | |---|---:|---:| | Bienville Avenue | 25 | 25 | | Banks Street | 23 | 22 | | Allen Toussaint Boulevard | 17 | 17 | | North Galvez Street | 38 | 36 |

The leading findings are therefore not driven by loosely located pins. Excluding municipal/USPS reports leaves 25 on Bienville, 23 on Banks, 16 on Allen Toussaint, and 38 on North Galvez. Bienville's 25 reports include 21 company-vehicle reports; Banks has 13 company-vehicle reports; this suggests delivery/loading practices are worth inspecting, without inferring a particular company or driver.

Limits on interpretation

  • These are potential enforcement gaps, not proof that officers ignored complaints. BLU reporting does not establish that a city enforcement request was submitted or that an officer observed the vehicle.
  • A vehicle obstructing a bike lane may receive another ticket code, move before an officer arrives, or qualify for an exception. Only the specific bike-lane parking code is counted here.
  • Reports are not a random sample of cycling conditions. Distinct dates reduce the influence of a single reporting event, but cannot identify unique reporters or deduplicate repeated reports of the same vehicle. IDs are retained and distinct; events are not assumed independent.
  • Dates use the source fields as supplied. The map does not explain whether every date is incident time or submission time. No hour-level response matching is attempted.
  • City ticket coverage ends July 31, 2026 despite a September model-refresh date. The latest bike-lane-coded ticket is July 26. Absence of later data is not zero enforcement.
  • Street matching is automated, using current centerlines. It does not confirm historic bike-lane layouts, and no official city-boundary polygon was applied. The named leading candidates are recognizable New Orleans streets; unmatched/outlier reports were excluded from their totals.
